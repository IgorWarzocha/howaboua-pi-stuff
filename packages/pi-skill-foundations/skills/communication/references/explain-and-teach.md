Resolve what the learner needs. A procedure teaches an action, a mechanism explains how something happens, and a lesson builds transferable ability. A why question may ask for cause, purpose, rationale, trade-off, recommendation, or justification. Ask only when different readings would materially change the answer.

Give the explanation a practical finish line. A procedure should let the learner act, recognise success, and recover from likely failure. A mechanism should let them trace cause to effect and predict what a changed condition would do. A lesson should let them handle a nearby case.

Lead with the shortest direct answer that remains true. Build from the learner's starting point through one concrete case. Define necessary terms where they first matter. An analogy can orient the learner, but the real relationship and any misleading boundary must follow it.

For procedures, keep the normal path in execution order. Put prerequisites and safety conditions before the action they govern, observable cues where success must be recognised, and explanation at decisions or irreversible steps. Separate optional branches and include recovery when failure is plausible or costly. Verify current, source-dependent, and safety-critical instructions.

For mechanisms, state the cause before its history and expose the causal chain. Do not replace mechanism with purpose, correlation, or restatement. Preserve multiple causes where one would be false simplicity. Separate evidence from interpretation, and use a counterfactual when it reveals what controls the result.

For lessons, use the smallest progression that reaches the intended use. Work through one representative example without skipping the core move. Give practice cues and feedback signals where reading alone cannot establish mastery. One prediction, rehearsal, or nearby application can reveal whether the idea transfers.

Match depth to the request and the learner's vocabulary. If the explanation misses, find the missing bridge and rebuild from one step before it rather than repeating the same shape with simpler synonyms.
